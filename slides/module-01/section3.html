Why search fails

Tokens don't understand meaning

Failed searches from Video 2
  • "backpack for Scotland" → 0 results
  • Colour filter "red" → misses fiery_red
  • "waterproof winter gloves" → misses products described differently
How BM25 / keyword search works
  • Tokenises text into individual terms
  • Matches exact tokens only
  • No understanding of synonyms or intent
  • Ranks by term frequency, not meaning
Every failed search in Video 2 happened because keyword matching can't bridge the gap between what customers say and how products are described.
BM25 is the algorithm behind keyword search. It's been the standard for decades. It works by breaking text into tokens and matching them. If your query contains 'red' and the product data says 'fiery_red', there's no match. If you search 'backpack for Scotland', it looks for those exact words in product names and descriptions. If the product says 'multi-day hiking rucksack suitable for Scottish Highlands', BM25 doesn't know those are related. This isn't a bug. BM25 is doing exactly what it's designed to do: match tokens. The problem is that customers don't think in tokens. They think in meaning.
The solution

Text → numbers that capture meaning

Similar meaning → nearby vectors. A search for "red" now finds "crimson" and "fiery_red" because they're neighbours in vector space.
Embeddings convert text into a list of numbers, a vector, that captures the meaning of the text. The key insight: things with similar meanings get similar numbers. So 'red', 'crimson', and 'fiery_red' all end up as nearby points in this space. 'Hiking', 'Scotland', 'backpack', and 'mountaineering' cluster together because they're semantically related. When you search with embeddings, you're not matching tokens. You're asking 'find me the things closest in meaning to what I said'. That's why 'backpack for Scotland' can match a product tagged for hiking and mountaineering, even though it doesn't contain the word 'Scotland'.

Three options on AWS for generating embeddings. Titan Embed v2 is the default choice for most workloads. It's Amazon's own model, available through Bedrock, and it's the cheapest option. 1024 dimensions gives you good quality without excessive storage. It supports 25 languages and handles English extremely well. Third-party models through Bedrock: Cohere Embed is the main alternative. It's stronger for multilingual workloads. There are also domain-specific options emerging. Higher cost than Titan. Open-source on SageMaker: for when you need full control. You can fine-tune on your own data, which can significantly improve quality for specialised domains. Cost-effective at massive scale because you're paying for compute, not per-token. But you take on the operational overhead of hosting. For Meridian, we'll use Titan Embed v2. It's the right choice for a UK retailer with 22,000 products in English. We'd reach for Cohere if Meridian expanded to Continental Europe, or SageMaker if they had millions of products and wanted domain-specific tuning.
