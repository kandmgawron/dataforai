Before we start

Who this course is for

✓ You'll get a lot from this if you...
  • Know your way around VPCs, IAM, Lambda, and at least one AWS data service
  • Are comfortable in Python and understand basic SQL
  • Know what a token, an embedding, and an LLM are — you just haven't built production systems with them
  • Are adding AI to an existing AWS stack, not learning AWS from scratch
↗ What you don't need
  • ML modelling experience — we are not training models
  • Prior RAG or vector database experience
  • CDK or infrastructure-as-code expertise (we use CloudFormation)
This is a 300–400 level course. If you're starting from scratch with AWS, come back when you've got the foundations.
Set the audience contract clearly and directly. Don't soften it. This course is for senior AWS practitioners and treating it as anything else wastes their time. "This course is for AWS practitioners at 300 to 400 level. You already know your way around the core services. You understand what a token is, what an embedding is. What you don't need is ML modelling experience — we're not training models here. We're architecting the data systems that make models useful."

What they already have

Meridian's current AWS stack

Walk through the architecture diagram. The goal is "you've seen this before" — it's a normal, healthy AWS stack. "Here's Meridian's current stack. ECS Fargate running the e-commerce monolith. Aurora PostgreSQL as the system of record. DynamoDB for sessions and carts. OpenSearch for keyword product search — tuned over years, works well for exact matches. ElastiCache for application caching. Kinesis into S3, Athena and Glue for analytics. If you've been on AWS for any time at all, you've seen this. It works. Performance is fine, costs are predictable, the team know how to operate it. But it's blind to intent. And that's where we start."

Every one of those services is a decision. Why OpenSearch Serverless and not pgvector? When does semantic caching pay for itself? How do you handle privacy when you're storing customer conversations?

The course doesn't tell you "use this one". It gives you the decision frameworks to choose well in your own context.

By the end of this course
  • You'll have built this architecture on your own AWS account
  • You'll have the decision frameworks to adapt it to your own systems
  • You'll understand the cost, failure modes, and trade-offs at every layer
Show the destination. Don't dwell on each service — just establish that we're adding a clear AI layer to an existing stack, not replacing anything. "Here's where we're going. Same Meridian stack underneath. Same Aurora, same DynamoDB, same Lambda. We're not ripping anything out. What we're adding is the AI layer. Every one of those services is a decision — and those decisions are what this course is about."
